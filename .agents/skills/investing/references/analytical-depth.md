# Explain earnings, cash and management decisions

Read this for substantive results updates or requests for deeper analysis. Keep the existing capture → reviewed facts → Excel → readable report → dated update flow. Add analytical depth where evidence supports it; do not impose page, chart or ratio quotas.

## Earnings quality

Compare like periods and reporting entities. Bridge prior profit to current profit through gross profit, other pre-tax movements and tax. Then unpack material movements from the notes: price/volume/mix only where the sales data support that attribution, costs, gains, impairments and tax recognition. An accounting bridge is not a causal price/volume bridge. Label aggregated residuals and explain material components rather than invent precision.

Compare reported earnings with operating cash, customer receipts, tax paid and investment needs. A closing-cash bridge is not a net-profit-to-operating-cash reconciliation. Show the latter only when its components have been verified. Cash conversion (operating cash / net profit) needs positive meaningful earnings and comparable periods; a high ratio alone does not prove durability. Do not call cash after selected capex distributable cash.

If illustrating the effect of removing an item, adjust both comparable periods and label the calculation a single-item sensitivity. Do not call it normalized earnings unless the other material adjustments have been reviewed. Deferred-tax recognition is not a cash receipt; its recoverability may itself depend on the commodity outlook.

## Capital allocation

Separate maintaining assets, growth projects, outside investments, financing and distributions. Preserve the full operating/investing/financing cash bridge so historical payments are not deducted twice. Distinguish expenditure, remaining commitments, carrying values and prospective funding; overlapping commitments and forecast capex must not be added blindly.

For material decisions, retain a short table: original dated commitment → latest evidence → assessment → next observable test. A commissioned asset can demonstrate delivery, but a management claim of on-budget delivery is not independently audited proof of return on capital. Measure acquisitions and minority investments against cash invested and attributable outcomes when accessible; otherwise say returns are unproven. Do not award management a numerical score or infer skill from a rising commodity price.

## Finish with a judgment

For each material question explain: what the evidence establishes; the strongest alternative explanation; what changes in the forecast (including explicitly unchanged); and what future observation would change the view. Compare with a prior forecast only if it genuinely existed before the result. Without sourced consensus, call it an investment hypothesis, not a proven disagreement with the market.

Use a profit bridge and cash-deployment exhibit when they clarify the question. Each must identify periods, currency, ownership, source pages and interpretation. The optional presentation-v3 Analysis sheet in the maintained Excel generator supplies formula-linked comparable history and bridge charts when two unambiguous comparable periods exist; v1/v2 remain supported. It does not add a new valuation engine. Inspect both Analysis print pages and verify its bridge residuals independently.
