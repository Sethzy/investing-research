# Analyst report references

Researched 20 September 2026. These are examples of observable analytical craft, not a verified ranking of investment performance. Inspect the relevant original sections before adapting a pattern. Borrow reasoning and organization, not proprietary prose, figures or old estimates. Source documents are linked rather than redistributed. Publication dates come from report text when available, not search-engine crawl dates.

## Framework references

- [Morgan Stanley Counterpoint Global: ROIC and the Investment Process](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_roicandtheinvestmentprocess.pdf). Public 31-page paper; expectations, free-cash-flow and competitive-strategy sections inspected. Use it to distinguish business returns from investor returns and connect margin/capital turnover to expectations. It is methodology, not a company initiation report.
- [Morningstar's Guide to Stock Investing](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/morningstars-guide-investing-stocks). Public methodology overview: competitive advantage, fair value and uncertainty/margin of safety. Adapt the questions, not proprietary ratings or a mechanical rating algorithm.
- [Aswath Damodaran: Commodity companies, value drivers](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/adamodar/New_Home_Page/littlebook/commodityvaluedrivers.htm). Public NYU teaching material on normalization. Useful for separating a commodity-price assumption from an asset-specific thesis; do not mechanically use past averages when scale or the business has changed.

## Additional decision-oriented examples, checked 20 September 2026

- [Bell Potter: Metals X, 22 July 2020](https://www.metalsx.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/20200722-Bell-Potter.pdf), especially PDF pages 8–10. Accessible report text inspected: old/new estimates, asset-by-asset valuation, forecast funding and diluted share count. The useful pattern is that project value and its financing appear together. Old assets, prices and assumptions are not current MLX evidence; issuer hosting does not remove potential broker conflicts.
- [Massif Capital: first-quarter 2021 investor letter](https://static.seekingalpha.com/uploads/sa_presentations/152/68152/original.pdf), PDF pages 6–7. Accessible original letter text inspected, hosted by Seeking Alpha. The Alphamin discussion tests economics below prevailing tin prices and distinguishes apparent cheapness from a clear catalyst. Adapt the downside and time-to-realization questions, not the historical recommendation or portfolio sizing.
- [Seeking Alpha: Alphamin, The High-Grade Tin Miner With A Low-Grade Multiple](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4894915-alphamin-resources-high-grade-tin-miner-with-a-low-grade-multiple). Indexed valuation passages inspected; direct article returned 403, so full analysis was not reviewed. Candidate pattern: explicitly disclose exit-multiple and discount-rate choices. Also a reason to test whether jurisdiction risk is counted twice; no endorsement of that model.
- [Seeking Alpha: Alphamin, Tin Scarcity Is Finally Converting Into Cash Flow](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4922504-alphamin-resources-tin-scarcity-is-finally-converting-into-cash-flow). Indexed summary inspected; direct article returned 403. Connect an apparent multiple discount to both operating cash and the reasons the discount could persist. Do not reuse its sector-median comparison without a suitable peer definition.
- [Morningstar: uncertainty rating](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/an-introduction-morningstar-uncertainty-rating). Accessible methodology inspected: uncertainty changes the margin of safety required. Adopt the principle, not proprietary rating thresholds.

The newly located Red Cloud NOA initiation PDF could not be retrieved; it is not a reviewed exemplar. These examples demonstrate report construction, not independently verified analyst performance. Seeking Alpha authors' opinions are secondary research leads and do not establish issuer facts.
